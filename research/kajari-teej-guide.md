## Persona (Stage 0)

It is Kajari Teej this morning and my mother-in-law has assumed I know what
that means. I have found forty pages telling me the tithi and a "vrat katha"
that reads like it was written for a pamphlet. What I actually need: when can
I drink water, what do I break it with, and is the neem branch a real thing or
something only my family does.

Read 31 Aug 2026: khatuwalebaba.in, mpanchang.com, rudraksha-ratna.com,

internationalnewsandviews.com, everydayvegcooking.com, amarujala.com,
svastika.in (author-supplied).

What almost every page carries: the tithi, a puja vidhi list, and a "vrat
katha" about a poor brahmin's wife and a moneylender, presented as though it
were scripture.

What only svastika.in says, and it is the whole differentiator:
**Kajari Teej has no Puranic katha of its own.** The stories in circulation
are folk narratives from oral tradition and printed pamphlets, untraceable to
scripture. The scriptural story underneath is Parvati's penance for Shiva,
Shiva Purana, Rudra Samhita, and it belongs to all three Teejs, not this one.

## Findings, with sources

**Date and tithi**
- Kajari Teej 2026: Monday 31 August, Bhadrapada Krishna Tritiya.
  (mpanchang.com, khatuwalebaba.in, divinestore.sanatanajourney.com,
  read 31 Aug 2026; three sources agree.)
- Tritiya runs 09:35 on 30 August to 08:50 on 31 August.
  (internationalnewsandviews.com, read 31 Aug 2026.)

**The fast**
- Nirjala, through the day AND the night. Some keep it phalahari instead.
  (khatuwalebaba.in, svastika.in, read 31 Aug 2026.)
- **It does not break at sunset.** It breaks after moonrise and after arghya.
  Kajari falls in the dark fortnight, so moonrise is late. This is the single
  most practically useful fact on the page and almost nothing surfaces it
  plainly. (svastika.in, academyofvedicvidya.com, read 31 Aug 2026.)
- Arghya: water with milk, roli and akshat, offered to the moon three times
  from a copper vessel. (khatuwalebaba.in, read 31 Aug 2026.)

**Neem**
- A neem branch is worshipped as Parvati, called Neemdi Mata, and receives
  water, roli, rice and flowers. Neem stands for coolness and protection,
  which is a monsoon concern. (svastika.in, divinestore, read 31 Aug 2026.)

**Sattu**
- The fast breaks with sattu first. (khatuwalebaba.in, svastika.in.)
- Roasted flour with ghee and jaggery. Grierson documented this exact
  preparation in 1885. (svastika.in, read 31 Aug 2026.)
- Five kinds are named: gehun, jau, chana, maida, chawal.
  (everydayvegcooking.com, read 31 Aug 2026.)

**Regional**
- Observed in Bundelkhand, eastern Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and Rajasthan.
  (svastika.in, read 31 Aug 2026.)
- Bundelkhand: gau puja before the first meal, chapatis with ghee and
  jaggery fed to cows. (svastika.in, khatuwalebaba.in, read 31 Aug 2026.)

**The three Teejs**
- Hariyali: Shravana shukla tritiya, green, swings, bangles, celebratory.
- Kajari: Bhadrapada krishna tritiya, named for monsoon songs of longing.
- Hartalika: Bhadrapada shukla tritiya, strictest, nirjala with a night vigil,
  the sand-linga story. (svastika.in, read 31 Aug 2026.)

## Answer in three sentences

Kajari Teej 2026 is Monday 31 August, and it is a nirjala fast kept through
the day and the night by married women across Bundelkhand, eastern UP, Bihar
and Rajasthan. It does not break at sunset like most fasts: it breaks after
the moon rises and has been offered arghya, then with sattu, roasted flour
with ghee and jaggery, a preparation documented in 1885. The neem branch
worshipped as Neemdi Mata is Parvati, and the katha most pages print as
scripture is folk narrative with no Puranic source.

## The gaps we fill

- Nobody says plainly that the fast cannot be broken at sunset.
- Nobody says the widely reprinted katha is not scripture.
- Nobody distinguishes the three Teejs in one place for someone who has just
  been told "it's Teej" and does not know which one.
- Nobody prints the honest position on moonrise, which varies by city.

## Open flags

  it is what Marwari households make. Nine sources read and not one uses that
  word. It may be a household or community term. Not printed as fact; asked
  back instead.
- No moonrise time is printed. The one figure the search surfaced was for a
  US city and would be wrong for every Indian reader.
