# research/kajari-teej-guide.md

Rewritten 31 August 2026 after the first version of the post shipped wrong.
Protocol: `loop/rulebook.md`. Two research agents dispatched: video practice,
and text/scripture. Video findings are from auto-generated Hindi subtitles on

## Persona

My saas has assumed I know what today is. I have found forty pages telling me
the tithi and a vrat katha that reads like a pamphlet. What I need: when can I
drink water, what is this thing in the tin my mother sent, and am I supposed
to be doing something with a neem branch.

## What the first version got wrong

1. Treated Kajari as one festival kept across Rajasthan, Bundelkhand, eastern
   UP and Bihar. **It is not.** Those are different rites sharing a name.
2. Said "a neem branch is set up", missing the talai entirely.
3. Treated sattu as food. It is bhog first, and it arrives from the mayka.
4. Carried a "what this page does not know" section, which is a bkl3 rule for
   a pricing site. See `loop/rulebook.md`.
5. Lectured. 1,196 words. No Hinglish.

## The bara — video, all read 31 Aug 2026

- Called **बारा / बारो** in Marwari, also **पिंडा / सत्तू पिंडा**. Both words
  used by the same speaker in one breath. (So Sweet Kitchen, Jodhpur,
  `b5aSlJ9p52o`, 2025-08-08, Hindi)
- ASR artefact worth knowing: YouTube's Hindi captions render बारा as the
  numeral **12** (बारह) in three separate channels. That mis-hearing is itself
  corroboration that the spoken word is *bārā*.
- **The grain decides who it is for.** Chana dal bara → the **daughter**. Rice
  bara → the **jawai/damad**. Wheat → **laddus for the children**. Stated
  independently in two videos. (So Sweet Kitchen `b5aSlJ9p52o` and
  `UC_Yu4gO274`)
- **It travels from the mayka to the sasural.** Made in the woman's parents'
  house and sent to her, **tied in red cloth with moli**, and she breaks her
  fast on it. (`UC_Yu4gO274`, `b5aSlJ9p52o`)
- Measure is **sava**: sava kilo (1.25 kg) full size, sava pav (~312 g) small.
  (Banna Banni Loksangeet, `yeQ2qb80uWE`, 2026-08-28)
- **Ratios disagree, and that is the finding.** Jodhpur, per 1.25 kg: 700 g
  coarse roasted chana dal, 300 g boora, 250 g ghee. Maheshwari samaj: flour
  3 : sugar 1.5 : ghee 0.75. No fixed ghee measure — "जब लड्डू बनने लग जाता है
  तब समझ लें कि घी परफेक्ट है". (`b5aSlJ9p52o`, SUNITA JAJU `Aitho7N6Ljg`)
- **Roast, then grind. Never grind then roast.** Her pinned reply to a
  commenter. Grind **coarse** so it does not stick to the mouth.
  (`b5aSlJ9p52o`)
- Monsoon detail: grain bought unpolished, not washed but **wiped with a damp
  cloth** and dried under a fan. Roasting 2.5 kg takes **two hours**, each
  grain separately, low flame. Blackened dal is picked out. (`UC_Yu4gO274`)
- **Thapna**: pressed by hand into a **कटोरदान**, the lidded round metal tin.
  That is the tin in the owner's photo. Set as a disc, not moulded.
- **बटलियां** alongside: five small pieces, one for the puja and four to give
  away after. (`b5aSlJ9p52o`)
- One household's output: 3 kg dal → two 1.25 kg baras, two 0.5 kg, plus
  batliyan. **Five hours.** (`b5aSlJ9p52o`)

**Decoration, and every element in the owner's photo is accounted for:**
silver varak laid on a **gota** disc then pressed into the centre; a **silver
coin** set in (Jodhpur; supari instead if no gota); a whole dried **coconut**
dipped in ghee first, *"otherwise the dry coconut sucks the ghee out of the
sattu around it"*; almonds and cashews with **one pistachio between each
pair**; **cloves and cardamom compulsory**, and the reason given is a
grandmother's: *"लौंग बुरी शक्तियों को दूर रखता है"*; a **swastik drawn
outside-in, never cutting through the middle**; saffron soaked and flicked
over the surface; food colour let down with **melted ghee, never water**; and
petals and leaves **modelled out of the sattu dough itself**. (`b5aSlJ9p52o`,
`Aitho7N6Ljg`)

**Not verified: dried rose petals.** Modelled petals yes, dry fruit yes, rose
petals no source. Not printed.

## The talai

- A miniature pond, **तलाई / नाड़ी**, embankment built **against a wall**, from
  sticky clay or **gobar**, on an upturned pata or the floor. Filled with
  **kaccha doodh and water**. (Meenakshi's Kitchen `XWrWuRg0l5U` 2024-08-21,
  `rrw8XNyL4JE` 2021-08-23)
- Clay is kneaded **like dough, deliberately stiff**, with **haldi and kumkum
  mixed in**, and must be built **in the morning** so it dries before evening
  or the milk seeps out. **A seat for the diya is built into the rim.**
  (Indu Mehta `1NY49DN37zg`, 2026-08-30)
- The reason, and it is the best line found: *"तलाई मतलब तालाब का ही एक
  प्रतिरूप… पुराने समय में महिलाएं तालाब में जाकर के ही यह पूजन करती थीं। अब
  शहरों में तो यह पॉसिबल नहीं है।"* (`1NY49DN37zg`)
- **The neem tree is not worshipped. Only the twig is used**, as an
  instrument. Explicit correction on camera. (`rrw8XNyL4JE`)
- The diya's placement is functional: a **long wick**, because the second half
  of the puja is looking at reflections in the water.

**[CORRECTED] The ghee-and-jaggery embankment.** One narrated source says it;
**zero of the videos that actually build the talai on camera show it**. The
borders shown are clay or gobar. Written as "some households" or not at all.
Earlier plan treated this as established. It is not.

**[CORRECTED] Panchamrit.** Appears **once**, in a national TV bulletin
reciting a generic pan-Hindu samagri list. Absent from all eight Marwari
practice videos, which consistently use **kaccha doodh**. The owner named it,
so it goes in attributed to households that use it, never as the norm.

## The reflections

The strangest thing on the page and no English source has it. You look into
the milk-water by diya light at the reflection of each object and speak to it.
Marwari formula: *"तलाई में नींबू दिख्यो, दिख्यो जैसो टूट्यो"*. Lemon first,
agreed by every source, then kakdi, fruit, the neem, the flame, pearls, nath,
maang tika, mangalsutra, bangles, the mehendi on your hands, the pallu.
**Four times each** (Bhabu Goswami `qTTWv1F78p8`) versus **seven** (Aarti's
corner `XgMrw69GQfc`, filmed as call-and-response between two women). Genuine
disagreement, both printed.

**The count of things looked at must be even**, and if you are short you add a
flower or a pearl to make it come out. One source. (`XgMrw69GQfc`)

Meaning is uniform: as these appear to me tonight, so may they still be mine
on my last day.

## Breaking it

- **Dhamodi/dhamoli**: a pre-dawn meal, explicitly compared to karwa chauth's
  sargi. One vlogger films hers at 3am. (`yeQ2qb80uWE`, `XgMrw69GQfc`)
- Arghya from the same lota that sat through the katha: moli at the neck, a
  swastik, **13 roli tikkis**, the 13 wheat grains dropped in. Couplet: *"सोने
  की सांकली, मोतियों का हार / चांद ने अरक देता, जीवो वीर भरतार"*. **Four
  circumambulations.** Number of pours not stated by any source. (`yeQ2qb80uWE`)
- **The vocabulary rule.** The bara is never cut and the word cut is never
  used. It is **पिंडा बढ़ाना** or **पासना**. Broken by a **man of the house**
  with a **silver coin**. Five or seven pieces, sources differ.
  (`XWrWuRg0l5U`, `EiherBnfNxE`, `C5NdGfuauYA`, `yeQ2qb80uWE`, `1NY49DN37zg`)
- **The aak leaf.** Sattu eaten off a madar leaf; then from a dona folded of
  aak leaves, **raw milk seven times, then water seven times**, saying *"दूध से
  धाई, सुहाग से कोहनी धाई"*; then the dona torn into four and thrown to the
  four directions. (`yeQ2qb80uWE`, part-corroborated `EiherBnfNxE`)
- Disagreement: recipe channels say **sattu first**; the vlog and the TV
  bulletin say **water from the husband's hand**, karwa-chauth style. Both are
  being done.
- **Bayna** to the saas: a small sattu, a blouse piece or saree, shringar
  samagri and cash, with a touch of her feet. Nanad or a Brahmani if no saas.

## Regional — the correction the first post most needed

- **Marwari/Rajasthani** owns the whole talai / Neemdi / bara / reflections
  complex. All eight practice videos are Rajasthani or Marwari-speaking.
  Neemdi is glossed *as a Marwari word* on camera.
- **Bundelkhand is a different festival on a different day.** भुजरिया /
  कजलिया: wheat sown in bamboo baskets on **Nag Panchami**, grown pale in the
  dark, placed on a brother's ears at Rakhi, immersed the day after
  Rakshabandhan. The **Mahoba Kajli Mela** attaches to this, not to Teej: its
  **845th** edition in 2026, commemorating a battle of **1182 CE**.
  (People's Update `cYupScoVYMU`, Good News Today `mMMOp6QfVjQ`)
- **Eastern UP (Varanasi/Mirzapur)**: mehendi, bangles exchanged, 16 shringar,
  saris from the sasural, nirjala, Shiv-Parvati, songs. **No talai, no Neemdi,
  no sattu anywhere in the footage.** (ETV Bharat UP `dJ7L6RvunGs`)
- **Bihar: nothing found.** Every Hindi search returned generic vidhi channels
  or Rajasthani content. Bhojpuri results are kajri *songs*, not ritual.
  Absence is the finding. No Bihari practice asserted.
- **A text-versus-video conflict not to smooth over**: web pages claim Neemdi
  Mata worship in UP and Bihar courtyards. No video supports it. Looks like
  SEO cross-contamination.

## Kajri geet

- Sung during the puja in Rajasthan too, on the jhoola. (`XWrWuRg0l5U`)
- **Two etymologies, both circulating, neither picked silently.** A performer
  at the Chunar Kajri Mahotsav traces it to **Kajla Devi**, an epithet of
  Vindhyavasini: *"मेरी विंध्यवासिनी मां जिनका नाम कजला देवी है… और फिर संयोग
  है, वियोग है, झूला है"*. (DD News UP `j7hMs09dXUM`, 2026-08-10) A TV
  astrologer gives the other: kajri = black, for the monsoon clouds.
  (`mMMOp6QfVjQ`)
- Alive and large: **~700 women** at Chunar 2026 under Padma Shri **Urmila
  Srivastava**. Against that, a woman in Varanasi on camera: *"अब यह लोग टीवी,
  मोबाइल में बिजी हो गए, यह सब छूटती जा रही है."* (`dJ7L6RvunGs`)
- Register is domestic and teasing, not devotional. The Varanasi festival song
  is "सैंया मिले लरकइया", a wife complaining her husband is a boy.

# Text and scripture strand (agent 2), read 31 Aug 2026

The agent ignored search summaries after finding they kept echoing svastika.in
back as if it were independent corroboration of itself. It downloaded and
grepped the primary texts instead. Everything here is from a text read
directly.

## The katha claim holds, and the hard version is much harder

**Kane, History of Dharmasastra Vol. V Pt. I (1958), "List of vratas", p. 278.**
The entry, verbatim:

> `Kajjali—Bh Kr 3 (by pūrṇimānta reckoning); tithi; Viṣṇu-pūjā; NS 123, AK
> (folio 274a, says it is on Śr. Kr. 3). NS says it is well-known in Madhyadeśa`

Kane catalogues ~1100 vratas and names each one's textual source. NS is the
Nirnayasindhu, 1612. AK is a manuscript of 1767-71. **No Purana.** Adjacent
entries on the same page read "(from Brah.)", "(from Bhav. U.)", "(from
Skanda)" — naming the Purana is his standard apparatus. Its absence here is a
specific blank in the one book whose purpose is to fill it, not vague silence.

Two things inside that one line nobody seems to have used:
- **It is a Vishnu puja.** Not Shiva, not Parvati, not a suhaag vrat. The
  oldest text naming the day says worship Vishnu.
- **"Well-known in Madhyadesa"**, the doab. Not Rajasthan, not Bihar. A Deccan
  jurist noting a north-Indian custom he does not keep, which is the signature
  of folk practice being noticed by scripture rather than descending from it.

**Twenty Puranas grepped for `kajjal`** via GRETIL. Every hit is the ordinary
noun, lampblack or kohl. Agni 78.43 lists it among things to donate; Narada
1.117.66 has women honoured with scent, lac-dye and kohl. Caveat printed
honestly: GRETIL lacks Bhavishya, Bhavishyottara, Padma and full Skanda. But
Kane had those and cites them constantly elsewhere, and did not cite them here.

**Not unique to Kajari.** Kane says Hartalika is absent from the Krityakalpataru
(12th c.) and Hemadri (13th c.) and survives only in digests from 1612 on, and
adds "It is difficult to say why the vrata is called Haritālikā". Only Hariyali
(as *Madhusrava*) reaches Hemadri. So: the Teej cycle is late, and Kajari is
the latest and thinnest of the three.

| | Tithi | Earliest text Kane cites | Deity there |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hariyali (Madhusrava) | Sr. su. 3 | Hemadri, 13th c. | noted as a Gujarat custom |
| Hartalika | Bh. su. 3 | Nirnayasindhu, 1612 | Uma and Shiva |
| **Kajari (Kajjali)** | **Bh. kr. 3** | **Nirnayasindhu, 1612, one line** | **Vishnu** |

**Underhill (1921)** has a full Haritalika entry sourced to the Bhavishyottara
Purana, and **no Kajari entry at all**.

**The moneylender story's own tells.** It weighs the dal in *sava kilo* — a
metric ritual quantity; Puranas do not weigh offerings in kilograms. And the
antagonist is a sahukar with a shop, stock and night-watchmen, and the story
turns on him choosing mercy over the law. Credit, theft and patronage: the
moral universe of a Marwari trading town.

**Where svastika.in overreaches**: it frames the Shiva Purana penance as "the
scriptural story underneath, belonging to all three Teejs". The chapter (Rudra
Samhita, Parvati Khanda 22, verified on wisdomlib) attaches to no Teej and
names no tithi or month. Fair inference, not a citation. And the oldest text
naming Kajjali calls it a Vishnu puja, which cuts against that framing.

## Grierson, verified verbatim

**Bihar Peasant Life (Calcutta, 1885), ch. XII, §1443, p. 399:**

> "In the north and east, on the 3rd Bhādon (August-September) occurs the तीज
> *tīj*, on which women fast a day and night in honour of the goddess पार्बती
> *Pārbatī*, and eat सत्तू *sattu* (ground parched grain) next day."

**He never uses the word Kajari.** He also does not say which fortnight. But
§1088 settles it from inside the book: he calls the new moon "the 15th Bhādon"
and shukla chaturthi "the 19th", so his month starts at the full moon,
purnimanta, so his unqualified "3rd" is the dark third. It is our day.

Also in §1443, and useful: for the *anant* he writes "on which day **both men
and women** fast", which proves he flagged joint fasts when he saw them. §1443's
tij was women only.

## Tod 1832 has the name, 53 years earlier

**Annals and Antiquities of Rajast'han, Annals of Haravati:** "On the **Kajri
Tij**, the joyous third of the month Sāwan, **when a Rajput must visit his
wife**, the vassals of Bundi were dismissed to their homes."

Tod attaches the Parvati-austerities origin to the *Sawan* (Hariyali) Teej in
Vol. I, not to Kajari. Independent 1829 support that the penance narrative
belongs to the cycle generally.

He also attended the Bundi Teej procession as the queen mother's guest, and
names **Ram Singh** and **Balwant Singh of Gotra** as present. The modern Bundi
legend says Balwant Singh of Gothra looted the golden idol from Jaipur and gave
it to Maharaja Ram Singh. Tod puts both men there, in the right generation,
without telling the story.

**Unresolved and printed as such**: Tod calls the procession he attended
"Sawan-ki-tij" and the Haravati festival "Kajri Tij", using Sāwan for both. The
agent tried to settle it by computing lunar phase for 1821-22 and could not.

## Crooke 1896: the surprise

**The Popular Religion and Folk-lore of Northern India** mentions Kajari twice,
both times as a **Holi-like monsoon fertility festival known for ribaldry**,
never as a fast or a women's vrat:

> "the indecencies of word and act practised at **the Holî and Kajarî
> festivals** in Upper India, which are both closely connected with the control
> of the weather."

The best Victorian ethnographer of eastern UP knew this day as something else
entirely. With the Nirnayasindhu's Vishnu puja, the picture is of a day
repeatedly re-purposed, and the suhaag vrat may be the newest layer.

## The calendar mechanism

The ambiguity affects **only the dark fortnight**, which is why it hits Kajari
and leaves the other two alone. Purnimanta cuts the month at the full moon,
amanta at the new. A bright fortnight lands in the same named month either way.
A dark fortnight is the opening of one month or the closing of the previous, so
it gets **two month names for one day**: Bhadrapada krishna tritiya (purnimanta)
or Shravana krishna tritiya (amanta).

Kane records the split **inside his own entry**: Bh Kr 3 by purnimanta, while
the Ahalyakamadhenu "says it is on Śr. Kr. 3". Four hundred years old.

Live consequence: hi.wikipedia's Hariyali Teej article says that in eastern UP
it "is celebrated as Kajali Teej", collapsing two festivals a fortnight apart.

**Practical**: Kajari is three days after Raksha Bandhan. Verified for 2026 by
moon age (~17.7 days on 31 Aug, three past full).

**Panchangs disagree on the tithi window** and both land the 31st by udaya
tithi: Amar Ujala 30 Aug 21:39 to 31 Aug 20:53; aggregators 09:35 to 08:50.
Do not quote a start time as settled. Times Now Navbharat prints "31 अगस्त,
मंगलवार" — 31 Aug 2026 is a Monday.

## Regional, corrected

- **Wikipedia is wrong** on the Kajali Teej Mela: it says bright half. The
  Government of India events portal (utsav.gov.in), ETV Bharat and Patrika all
  put it at Bhadrapada krishna tritiya, and the 2025 fair ran from 12 August,
  which is that tithi.
- **Jaipur keeps Hariyali; Bundi keeps Kajli.** Two royal processions a
  fortnight apart. "बूंदी में असली, जयपुर में नकली."
- **Bundelkhand cow worship: MEDIUM-LOW.** Widely repeated, but absent from
  Grierson, Crooke and Underhill, and the agent's searches kept returning
  svastika.in plus undated aggregators. Not printed as fact. Unchecked lead:
  Russell & Hira Lal, *Tribes and Castes of the Central Provinces* (1916).
- **East vs west is the sharpest contrast**: in the east the day attaches to a
  goddess temple and a song genre (Vindhyavasini, Mirzapur); in Rajasthan to a
  food and a suhaag vrat. Same tithi, two different festivals.

## Money

- **Sindhara belongs to Hariyali and to Sawan, not Kajari. HIGH confidence.**
- **Kajari's gift is the bayna. MEDIUM** — attested only in recent Hindi
  festival journalism.
- **"Satudi is named for sattu": HIGH.** **"Named for sattu sent from the
  mayka": LOW.** The causal claim is not printed.
- Grierson, Crooke and Underhill record **no** gift custom for this day at all.

## Who keeps it

Married women for their husbands, unmarried girls for a good husband, stated
identically everywhere. No regional variation found in *who*. No report of men
fasting anywhere. Two adjacencies: Tod's Rajput "must visit his wife", and the
Rajasthani udyapan directing gifts to younger male relatives.
