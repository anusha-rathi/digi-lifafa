# What people actually search, and who currently answers

SERP pulled 1 September 2026 via `scripts/serp.sh`, google.co.in, hl=en.
Twelve queries, twelve credits. Raw JSON per query alongside this file.

**On Keyword Planner.** It needs a Google Ads login this project does not
have, and its volumes come bucketed so widely they are close to useless for
choosing between two topics. People Also Ask is better for this job anyway:
those are questions Google has measured people asking straight after the
search, verbatim, and they are what an FAQ block should answer.

## The finding, in one line

Every life-event gifting query returns a shop. Not a thin article, not a
content farm. An actual shop. FNP, Vaaree, Shazé, Amazon, Etsy, Flipkart,
Meesho, Blinkit, Pinterest. Nobody is explaining the custom, because everyone
ranking is selling the object.

## What this changes about the plan

Two kinds of post, and they need different handling.

**Where the SERP is shops** (griha pravesh, baby shower, engagement, mundan,
kanya pujan): write the explanation nobody has written. Low competition,
genuine demand, and it is the site's actual subject.

**Where the SERP is Hindi news** (bhai dooj, karva chauth, navratri dates):
do not compete on muhurat. Amar Ujala will win that every time and should.
Compete on the money, which they never cover.

# Domestic worker bonus: the correction that matters most

Agent research 1 September 2026. Reddit, Quora and Facebook all blocked the
fetch tools, which removed three of the six SERP results, so the community
layer is a hole. Everything below is from sources actually read.

## The Payment of Bonus Act 1965 is repealed

**Code on Wages 2019, s.69(1)**, verbatim from the Gazette: "The Payment of
Wages Act, 1936, the Minimum Wages Act, 1948, the Payment of Bonus Act, 1965
and the Equal Remuneration Act, 1976 are hereby repealed."

All four labour codes in force **21 November 2025**. Bonus notifications under
s.26 followed **25 August 2026**: eligibility ₹21,000/month, calculation
ceiling ₹7,000 or the central minimum wage, 8.33% minimum, 20% maximum.

**So every People Also Ask in that SERP is answering a question about a dead
statute.** "Is my bonus 8.33% or 20%?" is about the wrong law, and was never
about a domestic worker anyway.

## The exclusion is definitional, not named

- s.2(m): establishment = a place where industry, trade, business, manufacture
  or occupation is carried on.
- s.2(k): employee = employed on wages **by an establishment**.
- A house is none of those. So not an employee, so no chapter reaches her. The
  twenty-person threshold in s.41(2) is a second bar you never reach.
- IR Code 2020 s.2(p) excludes **domestic service** from "industry" outright,
  achieving what a 1982 ID Act amendment never brought into force.
- **The wrinkle**: s.50(4) exempts an employer from register and wage-slip
  duties where he employs not more than five for "domestic purpose". Odd thing
  to exempt somebody from if they were never in scope. Unresolved, printed as
  such.

## Supreme Court, 29 January 2026

*Penn Thozhilalargal Sangam v Union of India*, ten unions, disposed without
relief. Held to be executive and legislative policy. Petition's own count:
**only 15 states and 2 UTs** extend minimum wage protection to domestic
workers, and domestic work is still not in the Central List of Scheduled
Employments despite a 2010 Labour Ministry task force recommending it.

## The saree question, answered from both sides

Workers: *"We don't want a sari or sweets. We don't want a favour, we want our
right."* (Geeta Menon, Bengaluru.) *"We don't want your jhoota mithai."*
(Sunita Rane, Delhi.) In 2015 in Bengaluru one member returned the sweets and
another turned down a saree, asking for cash. That started the campaign.

Employers, from a Delhi NCR piece in which **not one household gave cash**: a
water purifier, *"What good would a tea set or a casserole do for them?"*;
gold earrings and a TV; a recurring deposit account opened in her name.

Both sincere. They are not having the same conversation.

## Baksheesh versus bonus

Joseph, Lobo & Natrajan, **EPW Vol 53 No 45, 17 November 2018**, "Between
'Baksheesh' and 'Bonus'". The SERP hit is the shorter EPW Engage summary of it.

Joseph: *"The idea of baksheesh is very caste-based. The employer reasons that
she's doing punya by giving money. It's an unconscious feudal system at work in
an urban area... Workers are not beggars."*

The campaign: **year one a black ribbon**, explained only if asked. **Year two
a pamphlet** under the door. **Year three** many employers gave a cash bonus,
**but not a month's salary**.

## One month's salary is the demand, not the norm

Geeta Menon: *"The only challenge is to get them to give one month's salary.
Employers still don't understand why they need to do this."* The maid-agency
pages calling it standard practice in Mumbai are vendors.

## Chennai, 2025

Meenakshi, 18 years a domestic worker, 8 with the same household, ₹3,000 a
month. Asked for a ₹1,000 annual bonus. Fired. Recalled a week later because
they could not replace her at that price. Told she should be content with the
saree they gift her every Diwali.

## Bengaluru, 2023

An apartment WhatsApp message: *"use your discretion according to their salary
but not half month salary... No job in the world would pay us half months
salary as bonus."* From a complex where flats go for over two crore.
**Accidentally proves half a month was common enough there to organise
against.**

## Festival work is extra work, uncompensated

Sunita Rane: *"We deep-clean your homes before the festival and don't clean
ours because we have no time or energy left over. We leave our families and
come to your homes on Diwali day and work when you host your parties."*

A worker in Jaipur, from Mattila 2011: *"We also have a family and children.
We also have to celebrate the festivals, so what if we are poor."*

## The phantom statute

**"Domestic Workers (Registration, Social Security and Welfare) Act, 2008"** is
cited with section numbers by paycheck.in and The Leaflet. **It does not
exist.** Bills were proposed in 1959, 1989, 2004, 2008, 2015, 2016 and 2017;
none passed. The Leaflet cites its s.22 and s.23 and then says in the next
paragraph that the bill was never enacted. A bill promoted to statute by
repetition, and printed in the post as an example of the problem.

## The tax asymmetry

Labour law: a home is not an establishment, so she is not an employee, so no
bonus is owed. Tax law: s.192 has **no establishment test and no employee-count
threshold**, and explicitly does not care whether the employer is a company or
an individual. **The same woman is not an employee for the purpose of being
paid a bonus, and is an employee for the purpose of it being taxed.** Nothing
turns on it at these wage levels.

## And the correction to our own older post

**The Income-tax Act 1961 was replaced by the Income-tax Act 2025 with effect
from 1 April 2026.** Returns for FY 2025-26 still follow the old law. The
Diwali post cites s.56(2)(x) and Rule 3(7)(iv) by number; the thresholds appear
unchanged but the numbering is not. A caveat has been added to that post rather
than leaving a possibly-stale citation standing.

## Not established, despite being intuitive

- That the Diwali bonus funds the trip home. Confirmed for **factory labour in
  Punjab**, where 20 to 22 percent of the workforce leaves for Diwali and
  Chhath and employers offer bonuses to stop them. **Not** confirmed for
  domestic workers anywhere. Not printed.
- Kolkata's Durga Puja bonus custom. Zero sources.
- Any per-flat RWA collection figure, anywhere.
- Domestic workers quitting over an unpaid bonus.
